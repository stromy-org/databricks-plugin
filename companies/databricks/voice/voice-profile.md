# Databricks Voice Profile (L2)

Entity-specific voice for Databricks, layered on top of the L1 baseline
(`voice://baseline`). L2 adds to the floor; it never relaxes it. Recovered from
public materials (databricks.com) for internal showcase use — not an authored
Databricks voice document.

## Stance

Write as a data/AI platform authority speaking to technical and data leadership
audiences. Confident, precise, engineering-credible — the tone of a company built
by the original creators of Apache Spark, Delta Lake, and MLflow. Substance over
hype: back claims with concrete platform capability, not vague AI marketing.

## Language

English-first, technical register. Comfortable naming specific product surfaces
(lakehouse, Delta Lake, Unity Catalog, Genie, Agent Bricks) rather than speaking
in generalities.

## Do

- Lead with concrete platform capability and measurable outcomes (e.g. "60%
  latency reduction").
- Speak to a technical/data-leadership reader as a peer.
- Keep claims grounded in the product's actual architecture (lakehouse, open
  formats, governance).
- Stay calm and precise even when describing ambitious capability.

## Do not

- Use empty AI-hype vocabulary without a concrete backing claim.
- Talk down to a technical audience or over-explain basics.
- Introduce imagery or claims not traceable to Databricks' own public materials.

## Social register (measured from 27 X posts and 29 LinkedIn posts, captured 2026-10-06)

Source: @databricks (X) and linkedin.com/company/databricks, company-authored originals only; 13 retweets and 1 LinkedIn repost excluded, 0 replies. Window: roughly 2026-09-22 to 2026-10-05 (X) and 2026-09-24 to 2026-10-05 (LinkedIn). Claims are tagged MEASURED (with a count) or JUDGEMENT.

### Does social agree with "engineering-credible, substance over hype"?

- **Agrees. MEASURED:** 0 of 56 posts use hype words from a checked list (revolutionary, game-changing, unleash, supercharge, cutting-edge, seamless). 17 of 27 X posts and 16 of 29 LinkedIn posts name a specific product surface (Unity Gateway, Lakebase, Genie, Lakehouse and similar). Benchmarks are named when claimed (for example OfficeQA Pro v2, VectorDBBench, "in benchmarks versus another leading cloud data warehouse").
- **Agrees. MEASURED:** 0 hashtags on X; 1 hashtag in 29 LinkedIn posts (an event tag). No @-mentions on LinkedIn; 10 @-mentions across X posts, used to credit partners, model vendors or presenters.
- **Differs: more promotional and event-driven than the site register. JUDGEMENT backed by counts:** 14 of 27 X posts and 15 of 29 LinkedIn posts end on or contain an explicit pull-through verb (Learn, Watch, Read, Register, Download, Try, Explore, Listen). Webinars, eBooks, a certification discount and a stadium-sponsorship announcement sit beside the technical posts.
- **Differs: warmer and more direct. MEASURED:** 12 of 27 X posts and 9 of 29 LinkedIn posts address the reader as "you/your"; 9 and 6 use "we/our". Exclamation marks appear in 5 of 27 X posts and 6 of 29 LinkedIn posts ("IP Functions are now GA on Databricks!", "Welcome to the team"). The profile says "stay calm and precise"; social is mostly calm, but launch and partnership posts are openly enthusiastic.
- **Differs: emoji. MEASURED:** 9 of 27 X posts and 12 of 29 LinkedIn posts carry an emoji (function marks such as a lightning bolt, a book, a snake, a genie, a bear). The profile is silent on this.

### Post shapes and length

- MEASURED: median length 506 characters on X (range 151 to 850) and 594 on LinkedIn (range 113 to 851). All 27 X and all 29 LinkedIn posts are multi-paragraph. These are long for X; X and LinkedIn copy is largely identical (same post, same wording, link swapped).
- MEASURED: 9 of 27 X posts and 8 of 29 LinkedIn posts use a bulleted or arrow-list block (bullet, dash, arrow or check mark).
- MEASURED: 27 of 27 X posts and 22 of 29 LinkedIn posts carry a link (LinkedIn customer-story and keynote-slide posts sometimes carry none in the text).
- MEASURED: 5 of 29 LinkedIn posts are customer stories (named customer, outcome, link); 0 of 27 X originals are.

### Opening moves

- MEASURED: 6 of 27 X posts and 5 of 29 LinkedIn posts open with a question ("How much of your AI workload is really just making a decision?").
- MEASURED: 12 of 27 X posts and 12 of 29 LinkedIn posts open with a first line of 90 characters or fewer, usually a thesis, a launch statement ("... is now GA", "... just landed") or a reframing ("AI doesn't have an intelligence problem. It has a context problem.").
- JUDGEMENT: the usual pattern is a short claim or question, then the mechanism in plain engineering terms, then a list or a number, then a link.

### Cadence and topics (measured only)

- MEASURED: 27 company-authored X originals in about 14 days (about 2 a day) and 29 LinkedIn originals in about 12 days.
- JUDGEMENT (uncounted) topics on X: model-governance and gateway posts, Lakebase and Postgres engineering, Genie, new model availability, event and learning promotion. Customer proof is concentrated on LinkedIn.

### Calls to action

- Soft and informational: "Learn more", "Watch the full walkthrough", "Read the guide", "Register now", "See documentation". MEASURED: 14 of 27 X and 15 of 29 LinkedIn posts. No urgency or scarcity language was found beyond event dates.

### What the brand never does on social (within this sample)

- MEASURED: no hype vocabulary from the checked list (0 of 56); no hashtag stacks (0 on X, 1 on LinkedIn); no replies by the company account in the window; no em dashes on X (0 of 27) and 1 of 29 on LinkedIn.
- JUDGEMENT: no memes, no slang, no rhetorical hot takes; numbers arrive with a benchmark or a scope.

### Caveats

- A two-week window, dominated by a launch-heavy period; not a year-round norm. Engagement counts were not used for any claim.
- The X scraper returned 40 items of which 27 are originals; the LinkedIn scraper returned 30 of which 29 are company originals. Verbatim copy was verified against raw-x.json and raw-linkedin.json.
- Counts of emoji, "you/we" and CTA verbs are regex-based and may miss variants.
